FlatTrack: Eye-tracking with

Overview

Abstract

Existing eye trackers use cameras based on thick compound optical elements, necessitating the cameras to be placed at focusing distance from the eyes. This results in the overall bulk of wearable eye trackers, especially for augmented and virtual reality (AR/VR) headsets. We overcome this limitation by building a compact flat eye gaze tracker using mask-based lensless cameras. These cameras, in combination with co-designed lightweight deep neural network algorithm, can be placed in extreme close proximity to the eye, within the eyeglasses frame, resulting in ultra-flat and lightweight eye gaze tracker system. We collect a large dataset of near-eye lensless camera measurements along with their calibrated gaze directions for training the gaze tracking network. Through real and simulation experiments, we show that the proposed gaze tracking system performs on par with conventional lens-based trackers while maintaining a significantly flatter and more compact form-factor. Moreover, our gaze regressor boasts real-time (>125 fps) performance for gaze tracking.

Approach

Method Overview

01

Lensless Image Capture

A mask-based lensless camera (PhlatCam) is mounted on standard eyeglasses, capturing near-infrared (NIR) measurements of the eye region. The camera's ultra-thin profile (<1mm) allows placement in extreme proximity to the eye, eliminating the need for focusing optics.

02

Physics-inspired Reconstruction

A two-stage pipeline first applies physics-inspired processing using the known camera PSF (point spread function) to partially reconstruct the eye image from the raw lensless measurement, followed by a U-Net-based refinement stage for high-quality reconstruction.

03

Direct Gaze Regression

A lightweight convolutional neural network directly regresses gaze direction from the lensless measurements, bypassing full image reconstruction for faster inference. The network is trained on 20,000+ paired (lensless capture, gaze direction) samples.

Evaluation

Results

Our system achieves sub-degree angular accuracy on real hardware captures, performing on par with conventional lens-based eye trackers while being significantly thinner and lighter. The gaze regression network runs at >125 fps, making it suitable for real-time AR/VR applications. We validate our approach through both simulation studies and real-world experiments with our custom NIR–PhlatCam hardware prototype.
